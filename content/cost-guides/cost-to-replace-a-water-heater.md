Replacing a water heater typically costs **$900 to $4,500 installed**,
with most homeowners paying around **$1,800** for a standard 40-50 gallon
tank unit. Tankless systems cost more upfront but run cheaper over their
lifetime in homes with high hot-water demand.

## Cost by water heater type

| Type | Typical Installed Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Standard tank (gas) | $900 – $1,800 |
| Standard tank (electric) | $800 – $1,600 |
| Tankless (gas) | $2,000 – $4,500 |
| Tankless (electric) | $1,500 – $3,500 |
| Heat pump / hybrid | $2,200 – $4,800 |

## What drives the price up

- **Venting changes.** Switching fuel type (electric to gas, or to a
  high-efficiency condensing unit) often requires new venting or gas
  lines, adding $300–$1,000.
- **Code upgrades.** Older installations may need an expansion tank,
  updated shutoff valves, or seismic strapping to meet current code.
- **Access and location.** Units in attics, closets, or upper floors take
  longer to remove and install than a garage or basement unit.
- **Permit fees.** Many municipalities require a permit for water heater
  replacement, typically $50–$300.

## Ways to save

1. Get at least three quotes — labor costs for the same unit can vary by
   30% or more between contractors.
2. Replace before it fails. An emergency same-day replacement often costs
   more than a scheduled one.
3. Consider a like-for-like replacement (same fuel type and venting) to
   avoid the added cost of code upgrades.

## Do you need a permit?

In most U.S. jurisdictions, yes — water heater replacement involves gas or
electrical connections and is typically inspected. Check with your local
building department before hiring a contractor who says a permit isn't
needed.
