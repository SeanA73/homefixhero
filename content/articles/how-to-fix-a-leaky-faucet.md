A dripping faucet wastes more water than most people realize — a single drip
per second adds up to roughly 3,000 gallons a year. The good news is that
the fix is almost always simple and doesn't require calling a plumber.

## 1. Identify your faucet type

Before you buy parts, figure out what you're working with:

- **Compression faucets** (two separate hot/cold handles) almost always leak
  because of a worn rubber washer.
- **Cartridge, ball, and ceramic-disc faucets** (single handle) usually leak
  because of a worn O-ring or cartridge.

## 2. Shut off the water and relieve pressure

Turn off the shutoff valves under the sink, then open the faucet to release
any remaining pressure and confirm the water is off.

## 3. Disassemble the handle

Pry off the decorative cap, remove the retaining screw, and lift off the
handle. Keep the screws in a small dish — they're easy to lose.

## 4. Replace the worn part

- Compression faucet: unscrew the stem, pop out the old rubber washer, and
  replace it with an exact match from the hardware store.
- Cartridge faucet: pull the cartridge straight up and swap it for the same
  model number — bring the old one with you when shopping.

## 5. Reassemble and test

Put everything back in reverse order, turn the water back on slowly, and
check for drips at the handle and spout. If it still drips, the valve seat
itself may be worn and need resurfacing or replacement — at that point it's
worth calling a plumber.

## When to call a professional

If you've replaced the washer or cartridge and the leak persists, or if you
notice water damage under the sink, it's time to bring in a licensed
plumber before the leak causes further damage.
