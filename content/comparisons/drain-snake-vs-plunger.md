If you're standing over a clogged sink wondering which tool to grab, the
short answer is: **start with a plunger for anything near the drain
opening, and reach for a snake when the plunger doesn't clear it or the
clog is clearly further down the pipe.**

## When a plunger wins

Plungers work by pressure — pushing water (not air) through the clog to
dislodge it. That means they're best for:

- Toilet clogs
- Sink or tub clogs close to the drain opening
- Situations where you want a fast, zero-mess first attempt

## When a drain snake wins

A drain snake (or hand auger) physically reaches into the pipe to break up
or hook a clog. Reach for one when:

- The plunger didn't work after 2-3 attempts
- The drain is slow rather than fully blocked (a sign the clog is further
  down)
- You suspect hair or a solid object rather than a soft blockage

## Our recommendation

Keep both in your home toolkit — a flange plunger for toilets, a cup
plunger for sinks/tubs, and a 25-foot hand auger for anything that doesn't
clear on the first try. Combined, they cost less than a single plumber
visit and handle the vast majority of household clogs.
